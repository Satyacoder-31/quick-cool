AC Knowledge Center &
Frequently Asked Questions

Empowering homeowners and facility managers with accurate, honest thermodynamic knowledge. Understand why air conditioners fail, how to prevent high power bills, and how our engineers resolve faults.

🔍

If the indoor unit fan is blowing air but the room doesn’t get cold, the top four culprits are:

  • Clogged outdoor condenser coils: Dust prevents the unit from expelling indoor heat outside.
  • Low refrigerant gas: Caused by a micro-leak in the copper piping or flare nuts.
  • Faulty compressor capacitor: The outdoor fan turns, but the compressor pump never starts.
  • Thermostat sensor miscalibration: The unit falsely thinks the room has already reached 24°C.

Our technician will connect digital manifold gauges to verify suction and discharge pressure within 10 minutes of arrival.

When warm room air hits cold cooling coils, moisture condenses and drops into an internal plastic drain tray. Water leaks inside when:

  • The drain pipe is blocked by thick algae, mold sludge, or dirt.
  • The indoor unit was improperly mounted without a slight downward gravity tilt toward the drain side.
  • The evaporator coil is freezing due to low gas and overflowing when the ice thaws.

QuickCool clears blockages with high-pressure nitrogen flushes and corrects mounting slope using precision laser levels.

In high-dust, tropical metro climates, we recommend:

  • Filter cleaning: Homeowners should rinse dust filters every 30 days.
  • Comprehensive power-jet deep service: Every 3 to 4 months (minimum 2 times annually — before summer and post-monsoon).
  • Commercial units / offices: Servicing every 60 to 90 days due to continuous 10+ hour daily operational runtime.

Myth Buster: Air conditioners do NOT consume refrigerant like car fuel. An AC is a hermetically sealed closed circuit. If your gas is low, there is an active physical leak.

You should check gas only if: cooling takes over 45 minutes, ice forms on the thin outdoor copper pipe, or you hear a mild continuous hissing sound. QuickCool performs a nitrogen bubble leak test, fixes the puncture, and vacuum refills by exact gram weight.

A foul smell is typically caused by microbial mold and bacterial colonies growing in the dark, damp crevices of the evaporator coil and drain pan (often called 'Dirty Sock Syndrome'). In some cases, decomposing rodents or insects in the outdoor ducting cause acrid odours. QuickCool applies hospital-grade antimicrobial chemical foam that dissolves organic buildup and leaves fresh, sanitized air.

Different noises indicate distinct physical problems:

  • Rattling: Loose wall mounting bracket or loose front casing grill.
  • High-pitched squealing: Worn-out blower motor sleeve bearings.
  • Vibrating hum: Outdoor compressor anti-vibration rubber grommets have deteriorated.
  • Clicking noise: Electrical relay attempting to engage a locked compressor.

A standard residential split AC installation by a certified QuickCool 2-person crew takes approximately 90 to 120 minutes. This includes core wall drilling, leveling, bracket anchoring, copper line flare fitting, double-stage vacuum degassing, and 15 minutes of live temperature testing.

For rooms used more than 4 to 5 hours daily, an Inverter AC is vastly superior. Non-inverter ACs run at 100% capacity and repeatedly turn on/off, consuming high spike amperage. Inverter ACs dynamically throttle the twin-rotary compressor down to 20% capacity once the room cools, cutting monthly electricity costs by 30% to 50% while maintaining rock-steady temperatures.

Follow these 4 proven engineering tips:

  • Set thermostat to 24°C: Every 1°C increase above 18°C saves roughly 6% power consumption. Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) rates 24°C as ideal thermal comfort.
  • Run ceiling fan on low speed: Circulates cool air downwards, eliminating warm thermal stratification pockets.
  • Clean coils every 90 days: A dirty condenser forces the compressor to run 30% longer per cycle.
  • Block direct solar heat: Close heavy curtains on south-facing windows during peak daytime sun.

Consider replacing your unit if:

  • The AC is over 8 to 10 years old and utilizes obsolete R22 refrigerant.
  • The estimated repair cost (e.g. failed compressor or multiple coil leaks) exceeds 50% of the cost of a new 5-star inverter unit.
  • Your monthly electricity bills have climbed steeply due to irreversible compressor degradation.
